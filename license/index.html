How to become a licensed PE

Being skilled makes you an engineer at work. Being licensed lets you legally sign and seal drawings and call yourself a Professional Engineer. Here's the honest path.

The honest truth about skipping university

In the US, licensing is controlled by each state board, and most require an ABET-accredited degree. Self-study alone won't get you licensed in most states, but it can make you employable as a technician and get you through the FE. It also makes any degree you do later far easier and cheaper. Requirements change, so always confirm your route in writing with your state board before committing years to it.

  1. 1

    Learn the fundamentals

    Now

    Phases 1–4 of this roadmap. This is everything an accredited civil engineering program teaches, studied on your own schedule.

  2. 2

    Work in the field

    Month 9+

    Get hired as a drafter, technician, inspector, or survey tech. Log every week of experience with the name of your supervising engineer.

  3. 3

    Pass the FE Civil exam

    Year 1.5–2

    Computer-based at Pearson VUE, year-round. About 110 questions. The only reference is the NCEES Reference Handbook. Passing makes you eligible for Engineer Intern (EI/EIT) status.

  4. 4

    Complete an accepted education route

    Years 2–6

    Most states require an ABET-accredited engineering degree. A part-time or online degree while you work is the most reliable path, and your self-study makes it much faster. A few states accept long experience (often 8–12+ years) instead.

  5. 5

    Gain supervised experience

    Ongoing

    Typically 4 years of progressive engineering experience under a licensed PE after your degree (more on experience-only routes). Keep detailed records and references.

  6. 6

    Pass the PE Civil exam

    Final

    Choose a depth area: Construction, Geotechnical, Structural, Transportation, or Water Resources & Environmental. Pass it and your state board issues your PE license.

Three realistic routes

Recommended

A · Work + part-time/online degree

Work as a technician and earn an ABET-accredited degree part-time or online. Many employers (DOTs and large firms especially) offer tuition assistance. You never stop earning.

Limited states

B · Experience-only route

A small number of state boards accept many years of qualifying experience in place of a degree, sometimes with extra requirements. It's slow and the rules change. You may be limited to that state at first.

Middle path

C · Engineering Technology degree

An ABET-accredited Civil Engineering Technology (ETAC) bachelor's is often cheaper and more hands-on. Some states accept it for PE with extra experience years.
